# PJS — Proof, Problem Solving, and Integrated Organization

**Date:** 2026-10-03

## Candidate proof

The practical objective is not to declare oneself "the best." It is to build enough evidence that a demanding evaluator can see strong fit for the role.

For Jane Street-type quantitative work, relevant proof may include:
- quantitative research;
- mathematics / probability;
- coding and systems;
- experiments and debugging;
- machine learning where relevant;
- clear reasoning in interviews;
- collaborative problem solving;
- strong technical artifacts.

## Public Jane Street signals

Jane Street's own materials emphasize:
- asking strong questions;
- collaborative problem solving;
- problem-solving mindset for quantitative trading;
- porous boundaries between trading, research and technology;
- tightly integrated teams;
- relatively flat organizational structure.

This supports preparing for a high-standard environment where how a candidate reasons and collaborates matters as much as memorized finance knowledge.

## Point72 contrast

Point72 publicly describes its fundamental equities business as:
- sector aligned;
- multi-manager;
- composed of teams with meaningful autonomy;
- supported by centralized firm resources.

This creates a useful organization-design contrast with Jane Street's tighter integration.

## PJS operating implication

Do not prepare only for a company brand.

Prepare evidence for the actual organization:
- Jane Street: integrated problem solving and collaboration.
- Point72 / multi-manager contexts: role / team / PM fit, autonomy, investing process and accountability may matter differently.

The artifact stack should make those capabilities visible.
